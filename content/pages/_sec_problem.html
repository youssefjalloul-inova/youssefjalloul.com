Why pilots stall

Four failure modes explain most stalled AI programmes.

It is rarely the model. Industry research keeps landing on the same causes, and each one has a fix that is organisational before it is technical.

Failure mode 01

Nobody owns the outcome

AI sits between IT, operations and risk. Each assumes another is accountable, so pilots multiply without a business owner or a P&L target.

The fix: A named executive owner, a use-case portfolio with funding gates, and a monthly decision rhythm.

Failure mode 02

No data foundation

Pilots succeed on a hand-picked dataset and fail on production data that is fragmented, undocumented or not cleared for AI use.

The fix: A data-readiness check per use case before anything is built, and a register of permitted uses.

Failure mode 03

Governance arrives after the fact

Risk and legal review happen at the end, so promising pilots stall at the gate or ship without the controls customers and regulators now expect.

The fix: Risk tiers and human-in-the-loop controls designed in from day one, mapped to ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act and NIST.

Failure mode 04

Adoption is left to chance

The tool works but the workflow never changed. Usage stays low, shadow tools fill the gap, and the benefit never shows up in the numbers.

The fix: Workflow redesign, training and targeted behaviour-change interventions, measured on usage and outcomes.
