Meet the villain

Your AI lives in someone else’s house. And it talks.

Public chatbots (ChatGPT, Claude and the rest of the gang) are brilliant, cheap and everywhere. That's exactly the problem. Every prompt your team sends leaves the building and sits on someone else's servers, under someone else's terms, in someone else's country.

The toaster

It just wanted to help with breakfast.

Then engineers pasted confidential source code into it. Samsung banned staff from using ChatGPT and similar tools in 2023 after internal code was uploaded.

Bloomberg, May 2023
The microwave

Ding. Someone else's order is ready.

In March 2023 a bug let some ChatGPT users see other users' chat titles. For 1.2% of Plus subscribers active in a nine-hour window, it also showed names, emails and partial card details. Shared kitchen, shared leftovers.

OpenAI incident report, March 2023
The fridge

It remembers everything you put in it.

Prompts, files and usage logs live on servers you don't control, under retention terms that change when the vendor's lawyers do. Your fridge has a privacy policy. Did anyone read it?

Check your own vendor's terms
The fix

Don't ban AI. Own it.

  • Models that run on your servers or in an in-country cloud
  • Your data never trains anyone else's product
  • Governance built in, not bolted on after the leak
  • Arabic and multilingual by design
