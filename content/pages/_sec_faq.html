What does an enterprise AI strategy advisor do?
An enterprise AI strategy advisor helps leadership decide where AI creates measurable value, what to build, buy or partner on, how to govern risk, and how to move pilots into production. In practice that means use-case prioritisation, operating model, data and technology architecture, governance and change management.
What is an AI readiness assessment, and how long does it take?
An AI readiness assessment scores an organisation across strategy, data, technology, people, governance and change readiness, then ranks the use cases worth funding first. For a mid-sized organisation it typically takes two to four weeks and ends with a scored map, a prioritised use-case list and a 12-month roadmap.
What is a fractional Chief AI Officer?
A fractional Chief AI Officer is a senior AI executive engaged part-time on a retainer. They own the AI roadmap, governance and vendor decisions without the cost of a full-time hire, and can help define the permanent role when the organisation is ready.
How much does a fractional Chief AI Officer cost?
Market retainers for fractional Chief AI Officers generally run from about $3,000 to more than $30,000 a month, compared with full-time Chief AI Officer base salaries of $500,000 to $750,000 at public companies with 2,000 to 5,000 employees (Christian & Timbers, 2026). My engagements are scoped to your situation, and the intro call gives you a realistic shape and range.
When should a company hire a full-time Chief AI Officer instead?
Hire full-time when AI is core to your strategy, you already run a mature AI function and the role needs permanent ownership across the business. Most organisations benefit from a fractional leader for 12 to 18 months first, and boards should start the permanent search while the fractional leader is still in place because senior AI roles can take 50+ days to fill.
Why do most AI pilots fail to scale?
Pilots usually stall for organisational reasons rather than technical ones: no business owner accountable for outcomes, data that isn't ready or cleared for AI use, governance added late, and workflows that never changed so adoption stays low. MIT's Project NANDA found 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots delivered no measurable P&L impact.
How do you approach AI governance, ISO 42001, the EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF?
I build one auditable governance system first: policy, risk tiers, human-in-the-loop controls, vendor and model oversight, and board reporting. I then map it to ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework as they apply to your markets. This is advisory work, not legal advice, and I work alongside your counsel.
Is ISO/IEC 42001 certification mandatory, and how long does it take?
ISO/IEC 42001 is a voluntary, certifiable AI management-system standard, but enterprise buyers increasingly ask for it in procurement. It does not replace legal compliance work such as the EU AI Act, and certification commonly takes 6 to 18 months, so starting the governance programme early matters.
