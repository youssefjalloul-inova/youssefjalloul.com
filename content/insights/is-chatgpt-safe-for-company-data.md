**Short answer:** it depends on which ChatGPT, which settings, and what your people paste into it. The enterprise plans are a lot better than the free one. But the real risk isn't the plan you bought. It's the plan your staff are using without telling you.

Now the long answer. With a toaster.

## Meet Toastie

Toastie is a toaster. Eager. Helpful. Says yes to everyone, especially strangers.

Your marketing manager asks Toastie to "tighten up" the Q3 board pack. Your engineer pastes in 400 lines of source code to find a bug. Your HR lead uploads a spreadsheet of salaries to "make it look nicer". Toastie says yes to all of it. Toastie always says yes.

Toastie is not evil. Toastie just doesn't live in your house.

## What has actually gone wrong?

No conspiracy theories. Receipts only.

- **Samsung, 2023.** Engineers pasted confidential source code and meeting notes into ChatGPT. Samsung banned staff from using ChatGPT and similar tools, and said it would build its own internal tools instead. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-05-02/samsung-bans-chatgpt-and-other-generative-ai-use-by-staff-after-leak), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/05/02/samsung-bans-employee-use-chatgpt-data-leak))
- **ChatGPT, March 2023.** A bug in an open-source library let some users see other users' chat titles. For 1.2% of Plus subscribers active in a nine-hour window, it also exposed names, emails, payment addresses and the last four digits of cards. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/march-20-chatgpt-outage/))
- **The deleted chats that weren't, 2025.** In the New York Times copyright case, a US court ordered OpenAI to preserve consumer ChatGPT and API output logs, including chats users had deleted. That obligation ran until 26 September 2025. Enterprise, Edu and Zero Data Retention API customers were excluded. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands/))

Read that last one again. Your "delete" button is a suggestion, not a law of physics. When lawyers get involved, the fridge keeps receipts.

## Is ChatGPT Enterprise any different?

Yes, meaningfully. Business tiers come with admin controls, single sign-on, contractual commitments about training on your data, and they were excluded from that 2025 preservation order.

But here's the twist nobody puts in the sales deck: **buying the enterprise plan doesn't stop people using the free one.**

LayerX's 2025 enterprise report found 77% of employees paste data into generative AI tools, and 82% of that pasting happens through unmanaged personal accounts. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-employees-share-company-secrets-on-chatgpt/)) IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach report found breaches involving high levels of shadow AI cost an average of US$670,000 more, and 63% of breached organisations had no AI governance policy or were still writing one. ([Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2025/08/04/ibm-cost-data-breach-report-2025))

So you bought a secure kitchen. Your team is still making toast at the neighbour's place.

## What do regulators say?

In Australia, the privacy regulator's guidance on commercially available AI products recommends, as best practice, that organisations don't enter personal information, and particularly sensitive information, into publicly available generative AI tools. ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/guidance-on-privacy-and-the-use-of-commercially-available-ai-products)) Sending personal information to an overseas AI provider also brings cross-border disclosure rules into play.

In Saudi Arabia, the Personal Data Protection Law became fully enforceable on 14 September 2024, with a dedicated regulation for transfers outside the Kingdom. ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2024/09/saudi-arabia-s-personal-data-protection-law-become))

None of that says "never use AI". All of it says "know where your data goes".

## The 7-point checklist before anyone pastes the board pack

1. **Give people something better than the toaster.** Bans fail. An approved tool that's faster and smarter wins.
2. **Force company sign-in.** No personal accounts on company work. SSO or it doesn't happen.
3. **Classify what can go where.** Public, internal, confidential, crown jewels. Four buckets. One page.
4. **Keep the crown jewels at home.** Board papers, patient data, citizen data, source code: run them on private or in-country models you control.
5. **Watch the paste.** Browser and endpoint controls that flag sensitive data before it leaves.
6. **Write a policy humans will read.** If it's longer than a menu, nobody reads it.
7. **Keep the evidence.** Inventory, risk tiers, approvals, audits. When a customer or regulator asks "how do you govern AI?", you hand them a folder, not a shrug.

## The bottom line

ChatGPT isn't the villain. Using someone else's kitchen for your most private cooking is.

Use the cloud for the toast. Keep the family recipes in the house.
